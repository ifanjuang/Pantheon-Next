## Content summary

The note frames Ulysses as the phase of active recognition before method-building:

- Ulysses identifies phenomena and traps in our relationship to AI.
- Hercules, in the companion note, turns recognition into disciplined professional practice.

The Ulysses note covers ten traps:

1. false center;
2. uncontrolled abundance;
3. single viewpoint;
4. rushed request;
5. platform porosity;
6. coherence mistaken for fidelity;
7. unsituated memory;
8. seductive forms;
9. gain and its reverse cost;
10. output mistaken for judgment.

It also adds a practical case about AI agents in PME/ETI contexts, especially email/support automation. The example is used to show that an AI agent does not create an organization: it amplifies or exposes the organization that already exists.
